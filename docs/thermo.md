# thermo.py

`thermo.py` is the pure math layer for the three-state LOCKR model, cage closed, cage open, cage open with lucKey bound, drawn from Langan et al. 2019 and Quijano-Rubio et al. 2021. It never sees a sequence or a residue, just a `Kd` and a `pull`. The chain of functions: `theta()` gives the target-binder occupancy fraction (a standard Langmuir isotherm), `k_open_eff()` applies the allosteric `pull` to boost the open equilibrium once target is bound, `_f_open()` converts an open-equilibrium constant into the actual fraction of cages sitting in the productive open-and-key-bound state (a proper three-state partition function), and `fold_change()` divides the pulled state's `f_open` by the baseline `f_open` for the headline signal-over-background number. `max_fold_change()` gives the realistic ceiling at full target saturation for a given `pull`, set entirely by the cage, not by binder `Kd`, a tighter `Kd` only lowers EC50 and improves sensitivity. `diagnose_regime()` searches `K_open` and `lucKey/K_CK` independently for local optima and tells you which axis, if either, is actually worth engineering.

This file carries one real correction worth knowing. `_f_open`'s denominator used to add `luckey_ratio` on its own instead of multiplying it by `k_open`, which is wrong: key binding only happens after the cage opens, so the two equilibria multiply, not add (the same error, not a coding slip, was also in the original hand-derivation PDF). Fixing it changed real numbers throughout the project, ECLIPSE v1.0's fold-change at `pull=10` dropped from ~11.0x to ~7.41x, and flipped the regime classification from "key-limited" to "mixed": tightening either `K_open` or `lucKey/K_CK` now helps by a comparable amount, and the correct move is often to lower lucKey or tighten the latch, the opposite of what the old code always recommended. The source PDF still states the pre-fix numbers and needs a separate manual correction, this codebase can't fix a PDF.
