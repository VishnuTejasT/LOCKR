# charge.py

`charge.py` is plain amino-acid chemistry, no knowledge of LOCKR, cages, or lucKey at all; it would work identically on any protein sequence. `net_charge` sums the Henderson-Hasselbalch protonation fraction across every ionizable side chain and both termini at a given pH (7.4 by default), using a standard textbook pKa table. `helix_propensity` averages the 1974 Chou-Fasman P_alpha scale residue by residue to estimate how helix-friendly a sequence looks, and `helix_breakers` flags any internal Proline or Glycine, the two residues most likely to kink a helix (terminal P/G are excluded since a kink right at the end of a helix is usually harmless). `analyze_charge` bundles all three into one verdict: net charge, a `helical_ok` bool (propensity ≥ 1.0 and no internal breakers), and a note when something looks off. Cysteine counts as weakly acidic here (thiol pKa 8.5) alongside D/E/Y, not a typo.

On the real ECLIPSE binders, the charged original nets -6.13 at pH 7.4 while the neutralized fix (D/E→A) nets -0.13, and both come back `helical_ok=True`, the charge fix didn't cost anything structurally; helix propensity actually rose slightly since alanine is itself a stronger helix-former than the glutamate/aspartate it replaced. `liability.py` is what turns this raw chemistry into a LOCKR-specific "is this charge sabotaging K_CK" judgment; `charge.py` itself has no opinion on that. The ≥1.0 propensity cutoff and the terminal-exclusion rule for helix breakers are reasonable heuristics around the Chou-Fasman scale's own neutral point, not rigorously derived structural cutoffs, so treat `helical_ok=False` as "worth a second look," not proof the sequence won't fold.
